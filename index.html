Mechanical design of a drone driven by electric ducted fans. Counter-UAS: detection and capture

Two sides of one problem, as own work: finding a small drone in the sky with cameras, and a hand-held launcher designed to bring one down with a net.

DroneVision — two-camera drone detection on Jetson

  • YOLO11n · 2.6 M parameters
  • own dataset
  • seven training stages
  • 2× USB camera
  • NVIDIA Jetson deployment
  • desktop launcher

A small detector trained on my own footage in seven stages, then deployed to an NVIDIA Jetson with a desktop launcher. Two cameras watch the same sky; the status line reports how many drones are in view and which camera sees them, with detection running at 13–50 frames per second.

Final validation on 817 images with 592 drones: precision 0.92, recall 0.85, mAP50 0.90, mAP50-95 0.65, and about 1 ms of inference per image on the training GPU. Recorded outdoors against a mixed sky with trees, rooftops and sun glare — the conditions that make small targets hard.

NET-01 — disposable drone-net launcher

  • SOLIDWORKS 2025
  • 15 parts · 20-component assembly
  • 12 g CO₂ cartridge
  • 2 × 2 m net · 50 mm mesh
  • clash audit, revision B

A one-shot, hand-held pneumatic launcher that throws a 2 × 2 m net at a small drone 3.5–8 m away. The trigger pierces a 12 g CO₂ cartridge, the gas fills a receiver, and drives the packed net and its four 22 g weights out of the barrel at about 22 m/s.

The design basis came first, then the model: gas energy about 60–75 J, total mass 530 g. A clash audit of revision B took the assembly from 19 interferences to none. This is a design study — modelled and checked on screen, not yet built or fired.

Robotics and autonomous platforms

Three platforms at different scales: a large rover on hoverboard hub motors, a small Raspberry Pi robot, and a vineyard platform with a manipulator.

4×4 rover with a collaborative arm

  • 2× hoverboard hub motor, FOC
  • STM32F103RC
  • Pix32 V5 / ArduPilot Rover
  • Raspberry Pi 5 · Ubuntu 24.04
  • ROS 2 Jazzy
  • SLAM Toolbox + Nav2
  • Oradar MS200 LiDAR
  • NEO-M8U GPS
  • WITMotion BWT61CL IMU

Hybrid drive control: PWM from the flight controller plus UART from the Raspberry Pi, switching over when either channel fails. A separate strand of the work is reverse engineering the hoverboard controllers: identifying the microcontroller from the board silkscreen (GD32F130C6T6 on a PHC-018), pinout maps, SWD and UART wiring diagrams, and a firmware backup procedure to run before any write.

The SINGLE+UART builds for 10S and 7S compile cleanly. No board has been flashed and 42 V has never been applied — the work was deliberately stopped at the verification stage until the connector pinout is confirmed.

Vineyard platform

  • IIMT/EMT cobot, 5 kg · 900 mm
  • 10″ hub motors
  • ROS · gmapping · imu_ws
  • TCP socket control
  • 6-DoF pose datasets

A mobile platform with a collaborative arm for work inside a vine row: frame and chassis, controller cabinet, lidar and antenna brackets, and a gripper designed in house. Footage shot in a real vineyard gives the vision work its target scene — dormant vines, where the trunk, the canes and the trellis have to be told apart.

Small robot and the ROSCar PWA

  • Raspberry Pi 5
  • ROS 2 Jazzy
  • rosbridge
  • PWA, no App Store

The control and telemetry web interface is served by the robot itself, with a hand-written rosbridge client. It works from an iPhone or iPad through the browser. Profiles exist for several platforms, including a sensors-only mode for the Jetson AGX where publishing motion commands is disabled until the drives are deliberately connected.

04

Computer vision

Capture, calibration, detection, and turning a pixel target into robot coordinates — plus one discipline hobby projects almost always skip: safety interlocks.

Cup-Pick GUI with safety interlocks

  • PySide6
  • YOLO segmentation
  • Orbbec Astra Pro
  • OpenNI2
  • hand-eye
  • EMT/IIMT cobot

The pipeline: YOLO object detection, segmentation mask, depth at the target point, conversion into three-dimensional camera coordinates and then into robot base coordinates through the hand-eye matrix, generation of approach, grasp and retreat poses, and execution on the cobot.

The application starts with the camera and the robot disconnected, the robot in simulation, and motion forbidden. The arm cannot move until the operator connects it, turns simulation off, works through a ten-point checklist and ticks the motion-enable box. A second version is the same interface with the camera layer swapped for a stereo pair; 24 of 24 tests pass.

The limitations are stated rather than hidden: the Astra Pro has no hardware registration between the colour frame and the depth map — colour arrives over UVC, depth over OpenNI2, two independent buses; the hand-eye calibration is not finished; and the stereo calibration was inherited from a different pair of cameras.

Stereo depth on the Jetson AGX Xavier

  • 2× USB camera
  • OpenCV rectify
  • StereoSGBM
  • JetPack 5.1.6
  • 7×7 checkerboard · 28 mm
  • baseline ≈103 mm

Passive stereo is the only option that works outdoors: active depth, both structured light and ToF, goes blind in direct sun. Calibration on a 7×7 checkerboard with a 28 mm pitch, a baseline of about 103 mm, streaming 640×480 at 30 frames per second. Classical SGBM is confirmed on the Jetson; the trained model under TensorRT is not integrated yet — an open item, not a finished result.

Detection, datasets and CAD-based inspection

  • Ultralytics YOLOv8/9/11
  • YOLOv5
  • RealSense L515
  • Open3D ICP
  • Basler · FLIR · IDS · Zivid · GenICam
  • TCP I/O

The whole model lifecycle in one interface: frame capture, labelling, dataset splitting, training, live detection with a virtual line-crossing counter, and a panel for an external I/O controller over TCP. Separately, inspection of a real assembly against its reference CAD model: a point cloud from a RealSense L515, mesh loading, an inspection scheme generated automatically from the CAD, and comparison through ICP.
